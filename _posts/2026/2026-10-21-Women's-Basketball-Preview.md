The opener is tomorrow night against Tennessee Southern, and the roster that takes the floor at the Rollins Center will look almost nothing like the one that finished last season.

Three players are back from the rotation. All three were freshmen a year ago. Twelve of the fifteen names on the 2026-27 roster have never played a minute for this program.

That is the whole preview in one paragraph, but the interesting part is not how much left. It is what the record was actually hiding before anyone left at all.

<img src="{{ '/images/2026/WBB-Preview/uc-wbb-2026-slide-01.png' | relative_url }}" alt="Slide 1: Three players are back" width="700">
The story so far
Nineteen and twelve looks like a winning season, and it was one. It was also two completely different teams wearing the same jerseys.

In non-conference play, UC went 15-1. They beat Kentucky Christian by fifty. They beat Goshen 93-36, Union Commonwealth 83-35, Saint Mary-of-the-Woods 72-36. The one loss came at Marian, who finished ranked fourth in the country.

In the Mid-South, they went 3-9. On the conference road, 0-6.

The margins tell it more plainly than the record does. Overall scoring margin was plus 12.8 a game. In league play it was minus 5.0. Turnover margin fell from minus 0.8 to minus 6.1. Opponents made 7.2 threes a game against them in conference play while UC made 4.6.

Eight of those nine conference losses were by single digits or close to it. This was not a team getting run off the floor. It was a team that could not close against good opponents, and it was that team with its two best players still on the roster.

<img src="{{ '/images/2026/WBB-Preview/uc-wbb-2026-slide-02.png' | relative_url }}" alt="Slide 2: 19-12 hides a 3-9" width="700">
What comes back
Not much, and it is worth being specific about how little.

Of 2,352 points scored last season, 572 return. That is 24.3%. Minutes return at 27%, assists at 31%, rebounds at 19%, and blocks at 9%.

Aden Clark is the one to watch. As a freshman she scored 239 points, hit 34 threes at .351, and handed out 69 assists in 21 minutes a night, and she did all of it coming off the bench. She started three games in thirty-one. The minutes are there now whether or not she is ready for them, and the shooting profile says she is the closest thing this roster has to a returning scorer.

Jaydyn Carrico started twenty games and scored 255 points, but the efficiency has not arrived yet. She shot .363 from the field and .214 from three, with 70 turnovers against 40 assists. There is a real player in there. The sophomore year has to be the one where the percentage moves.

Noel Smith played 22 games and averaged 3.5 points and 4.2 rebounds. In conference play she shot .241. She also fouled out twice in fourteen minutes a night, which is a freshman problem that usually fixes itself.

What is actually gone
Joy Kendrick took 533 points with her, which was 22.7% of everything this team scored. She shot .492 from the field, .438 from three on 121 attempts, and .815 from the line, and she added 100 assists and 51 steals. In conference play she was better, not worse: 16.8 a game on .400 from three and .891 at the line. Replacing that is not a matter of finding one scorer.

Zaram Fredricks is the harder loss to solve. She pulled 344 rebounds at 11.1 a game and blocked 71 shots. The entire team blocked 137. One player accounted for 51.8% of them, and the 2026-27 roster returns twelve.

Lakin Burke added 216 rebounds, 86 of them on the offensive glass. Darcie Anderson contributed 83 assists and 47 steals across 24.9 minutes a night.

Last season's identity was the glass. Plus 15.5 on the boards, 46.5 rebounds a game against 31.1 for opponents. Between Fredricks and Burke alone, 560 of those rebounds are gone.

<img src="{{ '/images/2026/WBB-Preview/uc-wbb-2026-slide-05.png' | relative_url }}" alt="Slide 5: Fredricks blocked half the shots this team blocked" width="700">
What came in
Ten true freshmen and two transfers, and the recruiting was clearly aimed at the hole.

Nyajuok Chak arrives as a junior center from North Oklahoma CC at 6-6, the tallest player on the roster and the only newcomer with college minutes behind her. Skylar Elliott comes in at 6-5 out of Pallotti HS in Maryland. Olivia Onyemobi is a 6-1 freshman forward from Nashville, and Mya Yakubu arrives at center by way of Ghana and Frankfort HS.

Abby Varney transfers in at guard from Georgetown College, which UC plays twice.

That is four frontcourt bodies with genuine height for this level, which is a sensible response to losing a double-double machine. It is also worth saying the obvious: height and rebounding are not the same skill, and freshman bigs foul. The 55 blocks UC had in conference play last year came from a senior who knew where to be.

The schedule
Twenty-eight counting games plus an exhibition at Tennessee Tech in December, and the shape of it is the single most favorable thing about this season.

Nineteen of the twenty-eight are at the Rollins Center. The non-conference slate is thirteen home games out of sixteen, and the season opens with nine straight at home before the first road trip to Brescia on November 21. For a roster carrying ten freshmen, that is close to the ideal on-ramp. UC went 13-3 at home last year and 3-7 away from it.

<img src="{{ '/images/2026/WBB-Preview/uc-wbb-2026-slide-08.png' | relative_url }}" alt="Slide 8: Nine straight at the Rollins" width="700">
Then January arrives.

The Mid-South is brutal this year. Freed-Hardeman, Bethel, Campbellsville, and Lindsey Wilson have all been ranked inside the top eleven nationally, and UC plays each of them twice. Georgetown has been receiving votes and is also on the schedule twice. Add Marian at home in November and Spring Arbor in the Patriot Classic, and roughly eleven of twenty-eight games come against ranked competition.

UC went 3-9 in this league with Kendrick and Fredricks. The league did not get easier.

<img src="{{ '/images/2026/WBB-Preview/uc-wbb-2026-slide-09.png' | relative_url }}" alt="Slide 9: Then the Mid-South shows up" width="700">
Our take
13-15 overall, 2-10 in the Mid-South.

Non-conference should land somewhere around 11-5. Simmons, Virginia Lynchburg, Alice Lloyd, Union Commonwealth, Saint Mary-of-the-Woods, Cincinnati-Clermont, and Miami-Hamilton are all winnable at home with a young roster. Marian, Spring Arbor, and Tennessee Southern probably are not, and a team this inexperienced drops one or two it should have had.

Two and ten in conference is a one-game step back from last season, and that is the generous read rather than the harsh one, because 3-9 was the version of this team with its two best players on the floor.

Now the honest part. The error bars on this projection are enormous and anyone who tells you otherwise is guessing with more confidence than the roster supports. Ten freshmen means the range runs from 9-19 if the development curve is slow to 17-11 if Chak rebounds from day one and Clark turns into the scorer her shooting suggests she can be.

What we would say is this. Rick Reeves and this staff have built winning teams here before, the home floor is a real advantage, and the schedule hands this group nine games to figure out who they are before anything counts. Judge the season in January, not in November. A 2-10 league record with four freshmen playing real minutes by February would be a better season than a 5-7 that teaches nobody anything.
